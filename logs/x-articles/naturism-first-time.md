# The First Time I Got Naked Outdoors (And Why I Kept Doing It)

I still remember the exact moment the towel came off. It was a small clothing-optional beach, the kind you find at the end of a long path, and I had spent the previous hour pretending I was perfectly fine. I was not fine. My heart was going and my brain was running the usual script: everyone is looking, you look wrong, you should keep the shirt on.

Nobody was looking. That is the part nobody tells you.

## The fear is almost never about nudity

When people ask me what it is actually like to be naked around other people, they expect me to describe something dramatic. The truth is quieter. The first ten minutes are loud in your head and then, quite suddenly, they are not. You stop monitoring yourself. You stop adjusting. You notice the sun on your back and the sand under your feet and the fact that you have been holding your shoulders up around your ears for a decade.

That is the whole trick, if there is one. Nudity is not the point. The point is what happens when you stop performing.

## Bodies are just bodies

The thing that changes fastest is how unremarkable everyone becomes. You arrive expecting to compare and instead you stop comparing, because there is nothing to compare. Tall bodies, soft bodies, scarred bodies, bodies that have carried children and bodies that have carried grief. Nobody is auditioning. The person next to you is reading a book with their knees up, and the person on the other side is arguing with a friend about whether it is too early for lunch.

If you have spent your life feeling like the odd one out, that ordinariness is the gift. You are not the interesting one here. You are just another person on a beach.

## The rules are simple and they matter

A good nudist space runs on a kind of unspoken contract, and it is worth saying out loud.

- Ask before you photograph anyone. Always. Even a wide shot.
- Keep your eyes and your comments to yourself. Looking is normal; staring is not.
- Leave the place cleaner than you found it.
- Respect the space. Some beaches and resorts are family places and some are not, and it is not your job to blur the line.

Follow those and you will be welcome almost anywhere. Break them and you will be the reason a place stops allowing clothes-free visitors.

## Why I kept going back

The first visit was about proving something. The tenth visit was about the afternoon itself: swimming without a wet swimsuit clinging to me, drying off in thirty seconds, eating a sandwich in the sun with people who had stopped pretending a long time ago.

There is a particular kind of rest that comes from being around people who are not trying to impress you. I do not have a better word for it than freedom, which sounds corny until you have felt it.

## If you are thinking about trying it

Start somewhere small and legal. A clothing-optional beach, a naturist club with a day rate, a friend's back garden if you are lucky. Go on a normal weekday if you can. Bring a towel to sit on, water, and a book, and plan to stay longer than you think you will want to.

You do not have to be confident. You do not have to look a certain way. You only have to be willing to sit down, put the book in your lap, and let the first ten minutes pass.
