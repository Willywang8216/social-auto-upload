This Privacy Policy explains what information Socialupload collects, why it is collected, how it is used and shared, and the choices you have to manage, export, or delete information associated with your use of Socialupload.

1. Introduction

When you use Socialupload, you trust the service with media files, connected account information, publishing instructions, and operational records. This policy is intended to help you understand what information is collected, why it is collected, and how you can review, manage, export, or delete information connected to your use of the service.

Socialupload helps users upload, prepare, schedule, and publish content to connected social media and publishing platforms. The information processed depends on the features you use, the accounts you connect, the content you upload, and the settings selected in your deployment or workspace.

2. When this policy applies

This Privacy Policy applies to the Socialupload web application, backend services, scheduling and worker flows, account-management flows, support interactions, and related services made available through the Socialupload website.

This policy does not apply to third-party social platforms, cloud providers, payment processors, analytics providers, LLM or transcription providers, linked websites, or other third-party services that have their own privacy policies. Their handling of your information is governed by their own terms and privacy notices.

3. Information we collect

The categories of information collected depend on how you use the service and which features or integrations are configured.

  • Information you provide. This may include your name, display names, email address or other contact details if provided, account labels, workspace settings, profile configuration, and support messages.
  • Content you create, upload, or import. This may include videos, images, thumbnails, captions, titles, hashtags, transcripts, prompts, generated copy, campaign assets, and publishing instructions.
  • Connected account and authentication information. This may include cookies, OAuth tokens, API keys, refresh tokens, browser storage-state files, connected account identifiers, scopes, permissions, login status, and platform account metadata.
  • Publishing and workflow data. This may include selected platforms, selected accounts, scheduled times, queue status, post status, job attempts, retry history, logs, error messages, and platform response metadata.
  • Device, browser, and technical information. This may include IP address, browser type, device and operating system metadata, request timestamps, referrer URLs, crash information, server logs, security events, and diagnostic information.
  • Location-related information. The service may infer approximate location from IP address or receive location-related information from connected platform settings or account metadata. The service does not need precise GPS location for ordinary publishing workflows unless a configured integration separately provides it.
  • Information from third parties. Connected platforms and providers may return OAuth profile information, page or channel lists, account identifiers, public profile information, upload results, analytics, moderation status, or other response data needed to complete requested workflows.
  • Cookies and local storage. The service may use session cookies, authentication state, local storage, browser storage, and similar technologies to keep you signed in, remember preferences, secure sessions, and operate the application.

4. Why we collect and use information

  • Provide the service. We use information to support login, connected-account management, media upload, content preparation, scheduling, publishing, status tracking, and workflow automation.
  • Maintain and improve the service. We use diagnostic and workflow information to troubleshoot upload failures, monitor reliability, fix bugs, improve integrations, and understand which workflows need improvement.
  • Personalize and configure workflows. We use information to remember preferences, selected accounts, campaign profiles, platform settings, and other choices that make repeated publishing workflows easier.
  • Communicate with you. We may use contact information to respond to support requests, send security or service notices, and provide updates about important changes to the service or these policies.
  • Protect the service, users, platforms, and the public. We use information to detect, prevent, and respond to fraud, spam, abuse, unauthorized access, malware, illegal content, platform-policy violations, security risks, and technical issues.
  • Comply with law and enforce terms. We may use and retain information as needed to comply with applicable law, respond to lawful requests, enforce the Terms of Service, resolve disputes, and protect rights, safety, and property.

5. How information is shared

We share information only as needed to provide the service, follow your instructions, operate and secure the service, comply with law, or protect rights and safety.

  • With third-party platforms and providers at your direction. When you connect an account, upload media, request transcription, generate copy, store files, or publish content, the service may send the information required to complete that workflow to the providers you choose.
  • With service providers and processors. Hosting, storage, monitoring, security, support, analytics, communication, and other operational providers may process information for the service under appropriate confidentiality and security obligations.
  • With workspace or administrative users. If the service is used in an organization, shared deployment, or managed workspace, administrators or authorized team members may be able to access account configuration, content, publishing jobs, logs, and other workspace records.
  • For legal, security, and safety reasons. We may access, preserve, or disclose information if reasonably necessary to comply with law, enforce terms, respond to legal process, detect or prevent abuse, protect rights and safety, or investigate security or technical issues.
  • In business transfers. If the service or its assets are involved in a merger, acquisition, financing, restructuring, or sale, information may be transferred as part of that transaction, subject to appropriate protections.
  • When you publish publicly. Content you choose to publish may become public on target platforms and may be indexed, copied, shared, downloaded, or retained by other people and services according to the target platform’s settings and policies.

We do not sell personal information. We do not sell it for money, and we do not share it in exchange for anything of value. We do not use the information we collect for advertising or ad targeting, and we never sell Google user data.

Google user data

Socialupload offers optional integrations with Google services. When you choose to connect a Google account, the service requests only the scopes needed for the feature you are enabling, and it processes the resulting Google user data as described below.

  • YouTube publishing. If you connect a YouTube channel, the service requests the YouTube upload, read-only, and force-SSL scopes. It uses the returned channel identifier, channel title, access token, refresh token, and token expiry to display the connected channel, upload the video and metadata you select (title, description, tags, privacy status, thumbnail, and scheduled publish time), read back the uploaded video's status and URL, and confirm whether the upload succeeded. The service does not read, collect, or store your YouTube watch history, subscriptions, or private messages, and it does not modify videos you did not ask it to publish.
  • Google Sheets export. If you connect a Google account for sheet exports, the service requests the spreadsheets scope. It uses it to create and write to a spreadsheet you designate, containing the publishing, campaign, or analytics records you asked to export. It does not read spreadsheets you did not ask it to export to.
  • Google Sign-In. If you sign in with Google, the service requests the openid, email, and profile scopes and receives your Google account identifier, email address, name, and profile image URL. It uses these only to create and authenticate your Socialupload session and to show which account you are signed in as.

Google user data is stored on the service infrastructure described in section 10, is used only to provide the features listed above, is never sold, and is not used for advertising, profiling, or training machine-learning models. It is not transferred to third parties except to the service providers that host and operate the service on our behalf, or where required by law.

Socialupload's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

You can disconnect a Google account at any time from the Accounts page in Socialupload, which deletes the stored access token, refresh token, and channel metadata for that account. You can also revoke Socialupload's access directly at Google Account Permissions. To request deletion of Google user data held by the service, use the instructions in section 8 or the Data Deletion page.

To request a copy of your data, or deletion of your data:

9. Retaining your information

We retain information for as long as reasonably needed to provide requested workflows, maintain operational records, comply with law, resolve disputes, prevent abuse, protect security, and improve reliability. The periods below describe how long each category is normally kept.

  • Uploaded content and generated drafts. Kept until you delete them or remove the associated campaign. Deleting them from the library removes them from active storage; copies may persist in backups for up to 30 days.
  • Connected account credentials. OAuth access tokens, refresh tokens, and stored session cookies are kept for as long as the account stays connected in Socialupload. Disconnecting the account deletes them immediately.
  • Google user data. Google account identifiers, channel metadata, and Google OAuth tokens are kept only while the Google integration remains connected, and are deleted when you disconnect it. Exported spreadsheets are stored in your own Google Drive and are governed by your Google account settings.
  • Publishing and workflow records. Job records, queue history, and post status are kept for 180 days so that failures can be diagnosed and re-published, then deleted or aggregated.
  • Logs and security records. Application logs and security events are kept for up to 30 days, except where a record is needed to investigate an ongoing incident or abuse report.
  • Backups. Database backups are kept for up to 30 days on a rolling schedule, after which deleted records are no longer recoverable from them.
  • Legal and financial records. Some information may be retained longer when necessary to comply with a legal obligation, enforce the Terms of Service, resolve a dispute, or prevent fraud.

When information is no longer needed under these periods, it is deleted or irreversibly anonymised.
